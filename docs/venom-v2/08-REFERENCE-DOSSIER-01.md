# VENOM V2 — REFERENCE DOSSIER 01

Research batch: Utsubo / Noomo / Lusion / Argus Labs / Obys / Immersive Garden
Purpose: convert references into buildable VENOM principles.

## UTSUBO

Source:
https://www.utsubo.com/
https://www.utsubo.com/webgl-threejs-development
https://www.webgpu.com/showcase/utsubo/

Observed / published technical signals:
- real-time 3D;
- Three.js;
- WebGPURenderer;
- WebGL fallback;
- custom shaders;
- strong production focus on 60fps, compressed assets and deferred 3D;
- static/mobile fallback as a valid strategy.

Extract for VENOM:
1. Treat the serpent as real-time scene geometry, not a pre-rendered hero video.
2. Make lighting/material movement do much of the visual work.
3. Design performance and fallbacks before asset production.
4. Defer heavy scene loading so HTML/brand message arrives first.

Do not copy:
cheetah, exact camera path, exact materials, site choreography.

VENOM adaptation:
obsidian procedural body + authored head, WebGPU progressive enhancement, WebGL fallback.

Complexity:
HIGH.

Risk:
mobile GPU / thermal / asset weight.

## NOOMO — THE POWER OF DIGITAL STORYTELLING

Source:
https://noomoagency.com/insights/the-power-of-digital-storytelling-website

Published concept:
a glass phoenix guides the user through a scroll-built 3D world and transforms through the narrative.

Extract for VENOM:
1. A creature can be narrative structure, not a mascot.
2. Reveal the creature in states.
3. Transformation can connect brand metaphor to product/service meaning.

VENOM adaptation:
serpent begins physical, becomes data/particles at SNAKE, returns as controlled head reveal at close.

Do not copy:
phoenix, glass material, burn/rebirth plot, exact transition structure.

Complexity:
HIGH.

Risk:
narrative can become slow if every scroll segment is cinematic.

## LUSION — WEBGL SCROLL SYNC

Source:
https://webgl-scroll-sync.lusion.co/

Published purpose:
demo showing synchronization between WebGL visuals and DOM elements.

Extract for VENOM:
1. Keep copy in semantic HTML.
2. Keep 3D in canvas.
3. Synchronize them through measured scroll state.
4. Use explicit scene anchors instead of baking copy into 3D.

VENOM adaptation:
serpent crosses behind/in front of HTML headline while accessible DOM remains intact.

Complexity:
MEDIUM/HIGH.

Risk:
alignment across responsive breakpoints.

## STUDIO FREIGHT / ARGUS LABS

Source:
https://studiofreight.com/work/argus-labs

Published design clues:
- 2px square dot grid at 24px intervals;
- rare glowing “World Seed” artifact;
- near-black field;
- line-based sigils;
- pixelated Game-of-Life inspired cursor-following forms;
- website framed as manifesto rather than conventional marketing page.

Extract for VENOM:
1. Precious objects should appear rarely.
2. Technical grid gives precision without becoming dashboard UI.
3. A cursor field can make empty space feel alive.
4. Write like a thesis/manifesto rather than an agency template.

VENOM adaptation:
V/serpent is the precious artifact.
Lime is scarce.
Micro particles or cellular forms can react locally to pointer.

Do not copy:
sigils, gold palette, World Seed, exact grid composition or Game-of-Life expression.

Complexity:
MEDIUM.

## OBYS

Source:
https://obys.agency/about

Published identity:
typography + grid systems + motion used to balance clarity, usability and bold expression.

Extract for VENOM:
1. Typography must carry brand even without 3D.
2. Scale and spacing can create drama without extra graphics.
3. A strict grid makes experimental motion feel intentional.

VENOM adaptation:
giant architectural words, technical microcopy, asymmetric but measurable composition.

Do not copy:
house typeface or proprietary font files.

Complexity:
MEDIUM.

## IMMERSIVE GARDEN

Sources:
https://immersive-g.com/
https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/immersive-garden

Published stack/details:
- WebGL;
- Three.js;
- GSAP;
- Lenis;
- Vue/Nuxt in their implementation;
- 3D authored with tools including Blender/C4D/ZBrush/After Effects;
- explicit focus on balancing functionality and groundbreaking design.

Extract for VENOM:
1. Experimental layer must support real cases and technical proof.
2. Case studies deserve backstage/technical evidence.
3. Remove ideas aggressively; premium does not mean maximum effect density.
4. Lenis/scroll smoothing is optional engineering, not identity.

VENOM adaptation:
case-study chapters include a compact “system/proof” layer explaining what was actually built.

Complexity:
MEDIUM/HIGH.

# Immediate prototype questions

Before full design:
1. Can a procedural serpent body feel premium enough?
2. Can DOM text convincingly occlude with the canvas?
3. How dark can the material be before form disappears?
4. What is the minimum particle count needed for SNAKE transformation?
5. Can mobile retain identity with static key art + limited shader?
6. Does the hero still communicate VENOM in under 3 seconds before 3D finishes loading?

These six questions define Prototype Gate D.
